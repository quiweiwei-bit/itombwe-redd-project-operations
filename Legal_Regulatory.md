## 1. Executive Summary

This document provides an overview of the legal and regulatory framework applicable to the Itombwe REDD+ project in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Compliance with DRC law is essential for project approval, credit issuance, and long-term sustainability.

---

## 2. Key DRC Legal Instruments

### 2.1 Decree No. 113/MEF of 8 January 2019

**Title:** Decree establishing the principles applicable to the REDD+ process in the Democratic Republic of Congo

**Key Provisions:**
- Establishes the principles for reducing GHG emissions from deforestation and forest degradation
- Includes sustainable forest management, biodiversity conservation, and carbon stock enhancement
- **Recognises the right to generate and commercialise carbon credits** to natural or legal persons, public or private
- Provides the legal basis for carbon credit generation in DRC

### 2.2 Ministerial Order No. 047/CAB/MIN/EDD/AAN/MML/05/2019 of 9 May 2019

**Title:** Order establishing the procedure for mandatory homologation of REDD+ projects in the Democratic Republic of Congo

**Key Provisions:**
- Establishes the mandatory national homologation procedure for REDD+ projects
- Sets out the detailed standards for homologation
- Provides the regulatory and technical framework for REDD+ projects
- Defines the roles of relevant actors (CN-REDD, ARMCA, MEDD)
- Outlines registration procedures, approval requirements, and control measures

### 2.3 The 2022 Indigenous Pygmy Law

**Title:** Law on the protection of indigenous peoples in the Democratic Republic of Congo

**Key Provisions:**
- Recognises Indigenous Peoples' right to benefit from ecosystem services on their territories
- Requires consultation with Indigenous Peoples on matters affecting their lands
- Provides a legal basis for benefit-sharing with Indigenous communities

### 2.4 Ministerial Decree No. 026/2017

**Key Provisions:**
- Establishes the national framework for obtaining Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC) in REDD+ projects

### 2.5 Law No. 23/007 of 2023

**Key Provisions:**
- Amends the 2011 Environmental Protection Law
- Updates environmental governance provisions

### 2.6 Interministerial Order No. 006/120 of September 2023

**Key Provisions:**
- Specifies the allocation of national carbon revenue quotas
- **Does NOT specify a minimum share for Indigenous Peoples and Local Communities**

### 2.7 Carbon Tax on Forest Carbon Credits

**Status:** Under development

**Key Points:**
- The exact tax rate requires consultation with DRC tax authorities
- Up to **15% of authorised emission reductions** allocated as share of proceeds for national climate priorities
- Fiscal treatment being clarified as part of Congo Basin Roadmaps

## 4. Homologation Process

### 4.1 Overview

Homologation attests to the legitimacy of the project proponent in the eyes of the Congolese State and constitutes the right to monetise REDD+ performance on carbon markets.

**Process Steps:**

| **Step** | **Action** | **Responsible** | **Timeline** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Submit homologation application to CN-REDD | Project Legal Team | Month 0–1 |
| **2** | CN-REDD review and assessment | CN-REDD | Month 1–6 |
| **3** | Ministerial approval (MEDD) | MEDD | Month 6–9 |
| **4** | Receive homologation certificate | Project Legal Team | Month 9–12 |
| **5** | Secure Letter of Authorization (LoA) from DRC Government | Project Director | Month 9–12 |

**Total Homologation Timeline:** 6–12 months

### 4.2 Application Requirements

The homologation application must include:

1. Project Design Document (PDD)
2. Proof of project proponent legal status
3. Land tenure documentation
4. FPIC documentation
5. Benefit-sharing agreement
6. ESIA (Environmental and Social Impact Assessment)
7. Required fees

### 4.3 Letter of Authorization (LoA)

The Letter of Authorization is issued by the DRC Government authorising the international transfer of emission reductions under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement.

**Process:**
1. Submit project documentation to MEDD
2. CN-REDD review
3. Government issues LoA

**Cost Estimate:** Approximately **USD 3,000** for issuance

**Implication:** The LoA is essential for international credit transfer and sale.

## 5. Land Tenure & Carbon Rights

### 5.1 Legal Reality

**Community carbon rights are NOT formally recognised in DRC legislation.**

- Under DRC land law, the **State owns forests and their carbon stocks**
- However, there is also a view that carbon rights from REDD+ investments belong to those who invest in the project
- This includes project proponents, communities, and other stakeholders

### 5.2 Implications for the Project

| **Requirement** | **Action** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Carbon Rights** | Must negotiate with DRC Government (through CN-REDD/ARMCA) |
| **Community Rights** | Must negotiate benefit-sharing agreements through FPIC |
| **Documentation** | All agreements must be clearly documented in writing |
| **Land Tenure** | Must establish and document land tenure arrangements |

### 5.3 Benefit-Sharing Requirements

**DRC Legal Requirements:**

| **Requirement** | **Detail** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ministerial Order No. 047** | Requires project developers to negotiate benefit-sharing agreements with stakeholders |
| **Interministerial Order 006/120** | Specifies national carbon revenue allocation |
| **2022 Indigenous Pygmy Law** | Recognises Indigenous Peoples' right to benefit from ecosystem services |

**Best Practice:**
- Establish transparent, written benefit-sharing agreements
- Co-develop agreements with communities
- Include dispute resolution mechanisms
- Conduct annual monitoring and reporting

## 6. FPIC (Free, Prior, and Informed Consent)

### 6.1 Legal Basis

FPIC is mandated by:
1. DRC Ministerial Decree No. 026/2017
2. Equitable Earth standards (multi-stage FPIC required)
3. Verra VCS standards (safeguards requirements)

### 6.2 The 9-Step FPIC Process

| **Step** | **Action** | **Responsible** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Information, training and awareness sessions for communities | Community Liaison |
| **2** | Identify customary land and land rights holders | Community Liaison + Local Chiefs |
| **3** | Map rights, resources, land and territories | GIS Team + Community |
| **4** | Analyse local context; conduct socio-economic and land tenure studies | External Consultants |
| **5** | Identify and strengthen decision-making systems; assess gender dimensions | Community Liaison |
| **6** | Establish negotiation committee; begin agreement negotiations | Project Management |
| **7** | Strengthen leadership; address disagreements; establish conflict resolution committee | Community Liaison + Local Chiefs |
| **8** | Develop, negotiate, validate and formalise agreements in local language | Legal Team + Community |
| **9** | Establish independent oversight body for permanent monitoring | Project Management |

**Total FPIC Timeline:** 6–12 months

## 9. Critical Legal Reminders for Field Team

1. **Carbon rights are not automatic** — you must negotiate with the DRC Government
2. **FPIC is non-negotiable** — both Equitable Earth and Verra require it
3. **Homologation takes 6–12 months** — start the process immediately
4. **Benefit-sharing agreements must be documented** — in local languages
5. **ARMCA is not yet operational** — engage with CN-REDD in the meantime
6. **Tax rate is unclear** — consult DRC tax authorities early
