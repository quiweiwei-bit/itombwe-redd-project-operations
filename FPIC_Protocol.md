**Document Version:** 1.0

## 1. Executive Summary

This document outlines the Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC) protocol for the Itombwe REDD+ project. FPIC is a non-negotiable requirement under:
- DRC Ministerial Decree No. 026/2017
- Equitable Earth certification standards
- Verra VCS safeguards requirements
- UNFCCC REDD+ safeguards

---

## 2. What is FPIC?

### 2.1 Definition

FPIC means Free, Prior, and Informed Consent. It is a process through which communities have the right to:

- Be fully informed about the project in a language they understand
- Participate in decision-making
- Give or withhold their consent freely, without coercion
- Have their consent given prior to project implementation
- Have their rights and interests respected

### 2.2 Key Principles

| **Principle** | **Meaning** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Free** | No coercion, intimidation, or manipulation |
| **Prior** | Consent must be obtained before project activities begin |
| **Informed** | Communities must have full access to information about the project |
| **Consent** | Communities have the right to say yes or no |

### 2.3 Why FPIC Matters

- Protects community rights
- Ensures genuine community support
- Prevents conflicts and grievances
- Required for certification
- Builds trust and partnership

## 3. The 9-Step FPIC Process

### 3.1 Step 1: Information and Awareness

| **Objective** | **Action** | **Deliverable** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Educate communities about REDD+, carbon markets, and their rights | Organise meetings in local languages; use simple, clear language | Records of community meetings; attendance sheets |

**Key Activities:**
- Explain the project purpose and objectives
- Explain what carbon credits are and how they are generated
- Explain community rights under DRC law
- Explain the benefit-sharing mechanism
- Provide information about the project timeline
- Answer all community questions

### 3.2 Step 2: Identify Land Rights Holders

| **Objective** | **Action** | **Deliverable** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Identify all individuals and groups with customary land rights | Work with traditional authorities; conduct community mapping | List of land rights holders |

**Key Activities:**
- Identify traditional authorities
- Identify land rights holders
- Document customary land tenure systems
- Include all community members, including marginalised groups

### 3.3 Step 3: Map Rights, Resources, Land and Territories

| **Objective** | **Action** | **Deliverable** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Document community land and resource use | Conduct participatory mapping; involve community members | Resource maps; land use maps |

**Key Activities:**
- Map community lands
- Identify resource use areas (farming, hunting, gathering, fishing)
- Identify culturally significant sites
- Document land use patterns and seasonal activities

### 3.4 Step 4: Analyse Local Context

| **Objective** | **Action** | **Deliverable** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Understand the local social, economic, and cultural context | Conduct socio-economic studies; land tenure verification | Socio-economic assessment report |

**Key Activities:**
- Document community demographics
- Identify livelihoods and economic activities
- Assess community needs and priorities
- Verify land tenure status
- Identify vulnerable groups

### 3.5 Step 5: Identify and Strengthen Decision-Making Systems

| **Objective** | **Action** | **Deliverable** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Strengthen community decision-making structures | Work with traditional authorities; build community structures | Community decision-making framework |

**Key Activities:**
- Identify community decision-making processes
- Ensure women and vulnerable groups are included
- Build community structures for project governance
- Strengthen leadership and representation

### 3.6 Step 6: Establish Negotiation Committee

| **Objective** | **Action** | **Deliverable** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Create a committee to negotiate the project agreement | Work with communities to select representatives | Negotiation committee established |

**Key Activities:**
- Identify community representatives
- Ensure balanced representation (gender, age, groups)
- Train representatives on negotiation
- Begin agreement negotiations

### 3.7 Step 7: Build Consensus

| **Objective** | **Action** | **Deliverable** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Reach community consensus on the project | Address internal disagreements; hold community meetings | Community consensus documented |

**Key Activities:**
- Address any internal disagreements
- Resolve conflicts through dialogue
- Ensure all community members understand the agreement
- Document consensus

### 3.8 Step 8: Formalise Agreement

| **Objective** | **Action** | **Deliverable** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Formalise the FPIC agreement in writing | Draft agreement in local language; negotiate final terms; document consent | Signed FPIC agreement |

**Key Activities:**
- Draft agreement in local language
- Negotiate final terms
- Review agreement with community members
- Formal signature by community representatives
- Document the consent process

### 3.9 Step 9: Establish Oversight Mechanism

| **Objective** | **Action** | **Deliverable** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Establish independent oversight for ongoing monitoring | Create community oversight body | Oversight mechanism established |

**Key Activities:**
- Establish independent oversight body
- Include community representatives
- Provide training on monitoring
- Conduct annual evaluations
- Document all monitoring activities

- **Signed attendance sheets**

## 6. Key Principles for Field Staff

### 6.1 Do's

- ✅ Respect traditional authority
- ✅ Use local languages
- ✅ Listen to community concerns
- ✅ Answer all questions fully
- ✅ Involve women and marginalised groups
- ✅ Document everything
- ✅ Be patient — FPIC takes time
- ✅ Build trust and relationships

### 6.2 Don'ts

- ❌ Never coerce or pressure communities
- ❌ Never make promises you cannot keep
- ❌ Never bypass traditional authority
- ❌ Never exclude marginalised groups
- ❌ Never rush the process
- ❌ Never assume consent — always document it

## 7. Grievance Mechanism

### 7.1 Purpose

A grievance mechanism provides a formal process for communities to raise concerns and complaints about the project.

### 7.2 Mechanism Design

| **Level** | **Process** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Level 1: Local Resolution** | Community raises concern with local project staff; staff attempt to resolve |
| **Level 2: Mediation** | If not resolved, concern goes to community mediation committee |
| **Level 3: Project Management** | If still unresolved, concern goes to project director |
| **Level 4: External** | If unresolved, concern goes to independent third party |

### 7.3 Requirements

- Mechanism must be culturally appropriate
- Mechanism must be accessible (including to women and marginalised groups)
- Mechanism must be free
- Mechanism must be confidential
- Mechanism must be documented
